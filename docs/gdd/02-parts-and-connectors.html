Parts and Connectors

Status: partial

Spec

Robots are built from modular parts joined at connectors. A part is pure data: a physical shape, a density, power figures, a category, and a set of connectors. A bot design is a graph of part instances and connections that must form a tree rooted at exactly one core part. The design, not the assembled physics state, is the unit that gets saved, shared, and fought online.

Part categories

  • core: the mandatory root. Supplies power. Losing it disables the bot (combat slice).
  • structure: frames and plates that extend the connector surface.
  • mobility: wheels now; legs and other locomotion later. Attach via powered axle connectors.
  • weapon: damage dealers. Passive (spikes) now; actuated weapons later.

Core (chassis) variants

Players choose the bot's chassis from at least three core parts, each a distinct BattleBots-grounded body with its own connector layout, power supply, mass, and durability. The chassis shapes what a bot can be before any other part is placed. Cores are free (never sold); the workshop offers all of them.

  • Cube Core: the balanced all-rounder. Top/bottom/front/back rigid mounts plus two drive axles, power 100, durability 180.
  • Wedge Core (low invertible archetype): wide and low with a front-loaded weapon feed (two front mounts plus a top mount), lower power (90) and durability (150), low center of gravity.
  • Tower Core (tall brick archetype): a heavy multi-mount platform, five rigid faces plus two low axles, higher power (130) and durability (240), higher center of gravity.

Swapping the chassis in the workshop resets the design to a fresh bot on the new core (connector layouts differ), preserving the bot name and temperament, and is undoable. More archetypes (six-wheel control brick, two-wheel egg, big-wheel) are anticipated as later presets.

Connector kinds

  • rigid: fixed structural attachment. Becomes a fixed joint.
  • axle: powered rotating attachment with a local axis. Becomes a revolute joint with a velocity motor.

A connection joins one parent connector and one child connector of the same kind. Each connector accepts at most one connection. Prismatic (sliding) connectors are anticipated for actuated weapons but do not exist yet.

Validity rules (checked before any combat, REQ-002)

  1. Instance ids are unique and every part id resolves in the catalog.
  2. Exactly one core part.
  3. Every connection references existing instances and connectors, with matching kinds.
  4. No connector is used twice; no part has two parents; the core is never a child.
  5. Every part is reachable from the core (the graph is a tree).
  6. Total power draw does not exceed the core's power supply.

Validation returns design stats (part count, total mass from shape volume times density, power draw and supply) that the workshop UI will surface. Storage-backed workshop builds also pass an inventory budget: the core is free, while each non-core part instance needs one owned copy.

Duplicate part merging

Owned duplicate non-core parts can be merged into a selected part instance in the Workshop. Each merge level consumes one owned copy in the active design budget, caps at level 3, and increases combat durability while leaving connectors, mass, power, shape, and placement unchanged. Removing or undoing the design frees those copies back to the active build budget.

What this does NOT cover yet

  • Oriented attachment: parts currently attach with identity rotation at connector offsets (90-degree connector orientation steps are followup F-006).
  • Per-part durability, damage, and detachment (the combat slice owns those).
  • Capacity/slot budgets beyond power, part pricing, and rarity tiers (economy slices).

  • Drive gearing landed (F-229, fifth slice of the tinkerer-depth program). A connection on a drive axle can carry a gearRatio from 0.5 to 2.5: the pure gearedMotorCommand divides commanded shaft speed by it and multiplies motor authority by it, the torque-for-speed trade a reduction makes. Ratios are rejected on rigid mounts and spinner axles, where they would silently do nothing. Reduction draws extra power off the core, so torque competes with the weapon for the budget: the saw bot cannot afford maximum reduction. Ratio 1 is the exact identity (same hash-identity contract as the B3 temperament slice), so SIM_VERSION stays 5 and no replay was invalidated. Bench measurement recorded in F-237: the drivetrain is currently torque limited, so reduction still helps more than it costs at run time.
