Mining

Status: partial

Spec

Mining is the primary resource path (REQ-006): a deterministic 2D vertical grid rendered as low-poly 3D blocks from a side camera (Q-004 resolution). The player descends, digs, and decides when to turn back. The design below is the 2026-06-11 research pass (see docs/research/mining-mechanics.html and the baseline VibeReview findings F-012 to F-017): the goal is the "one more trip" engine of the genre's best mobile entries, kept fully deterministic so the server can replay any trip from (seed, gear, action log).

The trip loop (core, unchanged)

  • Every dig and move costs robot battery charge. Banking happens only on the surface, which also recharges the miner.
  • Carried loot drops as a recoverable bag on collapse (battery drained underground, falling rock, or fatal fall) or Abandon; banked loot is safe. Walking over the bag on a later descent restores its carried resources, support scrap, and parts. Falling-rock crushes leave the bag on the fallen rock's final rest cell so it stays visible above the rubble. If the cell supporting a dropped bag is mined away or picked up, the bag falls down the opened shaft until it reaches the next stable cell. The trip-back decision is the core tension.
  • Moving up through empty cells requires a cleared shaft AND ladders (REQ-020, user-directed change 2026-06-11; supersedes the implicit-ladder rule): climbing out of a cell consumes and plants a ladder there once, after which that cell climbs free for the rest of the trip. Pressing up into a solid diggable cell mines that overhead block in place without climbing or spending a ladder. After the overhead block opens, held up input must be released before the next up action can place a ladder. If clearing that block starts a falling-rock countdown, the mine gives an immediate warning sound and amber cue at the unstable rock.
  • Light: cells within lantern distance of the miner are fully readable horizontally and downward; zoom-out can render a short falloff band beyond that radius where real generated cells sit under a dark gradient. Stronger Lantern upgrades increase both the fully lit range and the useful overview, while far outer cells stay effectively black until approached.
  • In-world mine labels avoid runtime font atlas text. Exact text belongs in DOM HUDs, menus, dialogs, or overlays; the low-poly mine canvas uses mesh markers, icons, or controlled sprite atlases for cell-local indicators so mobile renderers cannot expose failed glyph quads as white squares.
  • Same seed, same mine, same finds: a trip is a pure function of (seed, gear, action log), and the server replays it to credit cash-outs.

Ore tiers and strata (REQ-011, REQ-012)

  • Seven ore tiers with roughly exponential total cell value and rarity inverse to value: coal, copper, silver, emerald, ruby, diamond, and core crystal. Ore swings roll deterministic small-unit bursts instead of paying a flat one chunk every time: a hit can chip nothing loose, pop one chunk, or pop multiple chunks based on ore tier, depth, and Pickaxe level. The cell clears only after its deterministic reserve ticks are depleted. Unit values stay small to balance five-high bag stacks, with the default ore curve at coal 1, copper 1, silver 1, emerald 1, ruby 2, diamond 3, and core crystal 4 vibes per chunk. Winter and high-tech resource variants keep the same early floor and pay up to 5 vibes for their deepest cores. Richer tiers carry larger reserves (4, 5, 7, 9, 12, 18, 28 base ticks) so total cell value climbs without turning one rich cell into a whole upgrade. Each tier lives in a depth band with overlap, so the previous tier stays present one band deeper and the next tier teases at a band's bottom edge. Past its main band, an older tier can still appear as a trace find; deeper ore cells multiply the reserve in authored steps through row 1,000 so old materials stay useful instead of disappearing.
  • Named strata start close together for the early lesson (Topsoil, Clay Beds, Old Granite, Glow Caverns, Magma Verge, ...), then spread into deeper authored landmarks through the row 1,000 push. Each stratum has its own dirt palette so descent is visible without reading the HUD. Reaching a stratum for the first time in a trip shows a banner, and deepest-depth progress fills cosmetic Stamp Book goals instead of adding bonus vibes.
  • The wallet currency is "Vibe-Brainiums" ("vibes" for short, user-directed 2026-06-15); ores convert to vibes when banked (Q-005 resolved). The DB column stays players.emeralds as an implementation detail. Banked part caches still grant robot parts (REQ-007).
  • A persistent deepest-depth record lives on the player profile.

Stamp Book achievements (REQ-032)

  • The pause menu includes a Stamp Book that shows the full mining achievement catalog, including locked stamps and progress. The first collection is mining-first and grouped into Depth, Haul, Tools, and Survival pages.
  • Stamps are cosmetic only. They do not grant vibes, consumables, parts, gear, luck, or combat power. The purpose is long-term pride and clearer goals without changing the mine economy.
  • Existing records backfill progress where the server already has durable data, such as deepest depth, gear levels, elevator rail depth, wallet history, owned parts, and active biome portals in the saved mine diff. Action counters such as bag drops begin from successful recorded gameplay after the system lands unless an existing profile field can safely prove them.

Device save slots (REQ-033)

  • Each device has three save slots available from the pause menu's Load game option.
  • Slot 1 is the compatibility slot: existing guest cookies and legacy local trip checkpoints migrate there the first time the new slot code sees them.
  • Loading an empty slot creates a fresh player tenant with a fresh mine seed and no copied progress. Upgrades, mined cells, collected stamps, purchases, bot parts, saved designs, consumables, wallet balance, and world diff all belong to that slot's player id.
  • The active in-flight trip checkpoint is slot-specific in local storage. Before server calls and before loading a different slot, the client saves the current slot's trip snapshot.
  • Existing slots can be permanently deleted from the Load game menu only after a second confirmation and a red destructive-action warning. Deleting a slot removes its server save data for mines, upgrades, stamps, purchases, bot parts, bot designs, bunker parts, bunker layouts, raids, wallet, and checkpoints, clears that slot's local in-flight checkpoint, and returns the slot to a fresh New game state.

Hardness and pickaxe gates (REQ-013)

  • Blocks have a hit count by material (user-directed 2026-06-12: dirt 4, cache 6, rock 5 at pickaxe level 1; each pickaxe level removes one swing, min 1). Ore uses the reserve rule instead: every swing is one logged action, dry hits spend one reserve tick, and multi-chunk bursts spend matching reserve. The ore payout for that swing can be zero, one, or multiple chunks. Better Pickaxes modestly improve ore burst odds and caps while still costing battery on every swing, each Pickaxe level above 1 adds a noticeable battery-charge cost to every powered strike, and richer ore tiers add their own battery strain so Battery Cell upgrades become attractive once ruby, diamond, and core-tier veins enter the route. Falling or fallen rocks and boulders keep a two-hit floor even with a maxed Pickaxe, so the warning window stays playable. Each swing renders progress as crack states, so rich resources can take 10s of hits even after the player has stronger pickaxes.
  • Rock comes in tiers keyed to pickaxe tiers, Terraria style: below the required pickaxe tier the block does not break at all ("granite, my pick can't cut it"); at tier, it digs in a few hits. The player should hit a visible wall one band before they can afford the answer.

Gear upgrades (REQ-013, REQ-014)

  • Persistent per-player tracks bought with vibes at the surface shop, progressive costs tuned against mined value: pickaxe (gates rock tiers, fewer hits), battery cell (max robot charge), cargo hold (carry capacity), lantern (light radius), plus specialized tracks such as Warpcoil, Blast Charge, Elevator Speed, Fall Harness, and Recall Rope. Early prices should be reachable in a few good trips, mid-depth prices should ask for deliberate deeper runs, and the row 1,000 transport goal should be expensive but finite.
  • Carry capacity is the secondary trip cap: the hold is a grid of typed stack slots. Each slot holds one resource type and up to five chunks, matching resources top off existing stacks first, and different resources never share a slot. A full hold no longer blocks digging, but any ore that does not fit falls to the nearest surface as a floor pile for later pickup with a full-bag flash and sound, so banking is still the only way to convert the carry safely.
  • Gear levels are part of the sim input. A trip is replayed with the gear snapshot taken at session start; the server validates the snapshot against owned gear at cash-out (Q-007). Buying a surface upgrade after a mined trip automatically banks that trip first, then applies the upgraded gear to the fresh trip immediately.

Hazards (REQ-015)

  • Falling rock (user-directed 2026-06-14: "rocks should fall a few seconds after the dirt beneath them is mined away"; tuned 2026-06-16 to "after two moves instead of one"; tuned 2026-06-20 to require at least two hits while falling or fallen): any rock or boulder cell whose support is dug out teeters in place, then drops after two player moves, crushing the miner if its falling path or rest cell covers the miner's cell (ends the trip like a collapse, carry lost, hauled up) or coming to rest as a path-blocking obstacle. The delay is counted in player actions, not wall-clock time, so the trip stays a pure function of (seed, gear, actions) and the server replay agrees; the escalating tremble is the tell, the hazard-free top rows never drop, and a falling or fallen rock or boulder can be mined when the player's pickaxe meets the rock tier for its current row, but never in fewer than two hits. Falls resolve as discrete grid steps from the action log: no physics engine and no reflex demand, since standing still is always safe.
  • Miner free fall (user-directed 2026-06-17): when support disappears or the miner walks into an unsupported empty shaft, the miner falls until landing on a filled cell, ladder, or plank. A base miner survives falls up to 4 cells; a fall of more than 4 cells ends the trip like a falling-rock crush, with carry lost and death-recovery ladder/plank stock applied. The Fall Harness upgrade raises the safe fall distance while staying part of the replayable gear snapshot.
  • Gas pockets: digging adjacent to a gas cell vents it; it detonates in a plus shape, destroying blocks (and their loot) and draining robot battery charge. Gas can chain into gas. Reads as a slightly-off block so depth knowledge becomes player skill.
  • Hazard frequency grows with depth; no reflex-based hazards (one-thumb friendly).

Consumables (REQ-016)

  • Bought at the surface shop, carried into trips, used as logged actions: dynamite (clears blocks through rock at any pickaxe tier and SCOOPS the ore and part-caches it breaks into the hold; ore past the hold's capacity stays piled on the floor to walk over later) and recall rope (ends the trip when the miner is inside the owned Recall Rope range, keeping carry, banking normally). Base rope range is shallow, and expensive Recall Rope upgrades raise the usable depth through the row 1,000 goal. All dynamite uses one stock count, but the HUD selects one of four blast tiers before confirming deployment: the base 1-cell plus, a long plus (2 up, 2 left, 2 right, 3 down), a 3x3 block centered on the miner, and a lamp-radius wipe for blastable cells. Stronger tiers are one-time Blast Charge unlocks at the Upgrades stall with premium prices tuned against the current mining economy, not repeatable radius upgrades.
  • Each resolves one dread: "I can't get through" (dynamite), "I won't make it back" (recall). Insurance converts anxiety into economy without deleting tension (F-015's fix).
  • Abandon (REQ-025, user-directed 2026-06-12): the free escape valve under both. Below ground, no consumable, two-tap confirm: the crew hauls you up and the carry stays behind, exactly a collapse but chosen. Being out of ladders and rope is a loss of cargo, never a softlock of time.

Ladders and planks: traversal aids (REQ-020, REQ-022)

  • Ladders are bought at the shop (2 cr); trips no longer ship a free bundle (user-directed 2026-06-15). A brand-new player is gifted one starting bundle (8 ladders, 4 planks) at account creation so the first descent works, but it is a one-time gift, never re-granted on selling. After that the one free source is dying in the mine (battery out or crushed, not giving up): a death tops the ladder stock back up TO 8 so the miner can climb out. Free death-rungs do not bank between trips; only purchased ladders carry over.
  • Planted ladders persist in the cell and render in the shaft. Players remove them through edit pickup mode: tap or click eligible placed ladder cells, mark them in-world, then confirm one logged salvage action. Salvage pays partial carried vibe value instead of returning ladder inventory. If the cell below a ladder is mined away or a supporting lower ladder is salvaged, the unsupported ladder slides down the opened shaft until it lands on solid ground or another ladder. A falling boulder smashes any ladder in the cell it lands in. Ladder placement is blocked while riding the elevator rail.
  • Out of ladders below ground, the options are honest: buy more next trip, spend a recall rope now if the miner is within the owned rope range, give up the carry with Abandon, or run the robot battery dry and lose the carry (and get refilled to 8 on the death). The HUD prices the climb home in charge AND ladders, warning when either is short.
  • Server replay parity: ladder spend is part of the deterministic action log; cash-out decrements the purchased portion (used minus what deaths granted this trip, floored at zero), so death-refilled rungs are never charged.
  • Planks (REQ-022, user-directed 2026-06-16; supersedes the auto-place rule from 2026-06-11): planks deploy only from the explicit plank button, in the miner's facing direction. The button is disabled without plank stock, while riding the elevator rail, or when the facing cell and its below cell are both non-empty. If the facing cell is solid but the cell below is empty, the player can pre-place the plank on that solid cell; when that block is mined, the plank remains and the miner walks onto it without falling. Without a support, lateral movement can still enter the cell and then falls through empty cells until supported, or dies if the fall exceeds the current Fall Harness limit. Visible ladders and planks can be salvaged only from the miner's adjacent 3x3 range by tapping or clicking their cells in salvage mode, then confirming the selected pickup. Salvage removes the support and adds a carried partial vibe value instead of returning a consumable, so the value is still lost if the miner dies before surfacing. A plank can also be broken by repeated down hits with the pickaxe while standing on it; the final hit removes the plank and adds the same partial salvage value. Planks are bought at the shop (2 cr) with no per-trip freebie; a death refills plank stock up to 4 the same way ladders refill to 8. Boulders smash planks like ladders; the surface walk row is boardwalked (village walking never needs planks). Same death-grant carryover and replay-parity rules as ladders. Ladders count as support (fixed 2026-06-12, user-reported): a ladder in the target cell is held onto and one in the cell below tops out underfoot, so no plank is needed over a laddered shaft.

Touch-first controls (REQ-023)

  • One-thumb play: pressing anywhere on the mine view spawns a virtual thumbstick under the finger (VibeKit's virtual-joystick module); pushing past the deadzone fires the dominant cardinal direction immediately, then repeats while held. Release dismisses it. Buttons, stall menus, and panels stack above the touch surface and keep their normal taps. Fresh taps, quick key presses, and new stick directions execute immediately. Held keys and held thumbstick movement repeat through a short pickaxe-scaled cadence so movement and mining feel responsive without held-input spam.
  • Camera zoom is a render preference, not a sim input: scroll wheel, two-finger pinch, and trigger or shoulder plus D-pad gamepad input adjust it. Zoomed cells use the real deterministic mine generation and sparse diff, so a distant dark-edge cell is the same cell the miner reaches later.
  • The dynamite HUD opens a compact tier selector. All four tiers are visible before ownership; selecting any tier previews the cells it can destroy by brightening them in the mine view. Locked tiers disable the confirm check and explain that the upgrade can be purchased at the Upgrades stall.
  • Game text never selects: long-presses and drags must not open the platform copy/share callout. Editable fields (design names) opt back into selection.
  • Control copy is pointer-aware (user-directed 2026-06-12): coarse-pointer devices never see keyboard mentions; the onboarding hint reads "drag anywhere to move" on touch and adds the WASD note only on fine pointers.
  • HUD discipline (REQ-024): glanceable chips, not paragraphs. Depth and stratum, a battery-charge bar, one bag capacity chip, and the climb cost (charge + ladders, red when short) live in thin chips; tapping the bag opens a scrollable visual grid where capacity is represented by stack slots. Each occupied slot renders the carried resource graphic, the count in that stack, and a darker overlay when the stack is maxed at five. Tapping a stack selects that whole stack, and Drop selected leaves the selected stacks on the current underground cell to make room. Manual bag drops merge with existing floor piles, but walk-over pickup takes older pile contents before reclaiming chunks the player just dropped; opening the bag while standing on a pile never auto-picks from that cell until the player moves away and back. Action feedback is a single one-line toast; consumables are icon buttons in thumb reach; there is no page header, no D-pad, and no standing help text (a one-shot "drag to move" hint disappears after the first action). Banked haul auto-sells when the miner reaches the surface, so the HUD shows one spendable vibe wallet instead of a separate banked-vibes counter. Sim numbers ride data attributes so tests never pin chip copy.
  • Surface game tips are part of the versioned HUD copy. Every package version bump reviews the tip rotation, removes stale advice, and updates tips for changed mine rules so the surface never repeats outdated guidance.
  • HUD vibes and climb readout (user-directed 2026-06-16): the spendable vibe wallet is always shown as the leading chip (it used to appear only inside an open stall sheet), and the cryptic combined climb-cost chip (up-arrow, charge bolt, ladder) was removed; the battery bar and the ladder count still carry that information.
  • Feedback collection (REQ-040): the pause menu includes a Feedback option with common categories, a free comment field, and an optional email field. The ladder-gravity mechanic can trigger a prompt after its fall animation settles, with choices to acknowledge, give feedback immediately, or never show that prompt again. Submitted feedback stores with the active player id and a reviewed flag for later operator review.
  • Performance samples (REQ-041): the mine collects lightweight browser frame samples from real sessions so old-device visual glitches and low frame rates can be diagnosed by app version, mine version, renderer mode, viewport, device pixel ratio, hardware hints, draw calls, and frame-time percentiles. Sampling must not alter deterministic mine simulation or add visible UI.

The surface village (REQ-021)

  • The surface row is a place, not a menu bar: the miner walks between shop stalls drawn in the scene (Hardware Store at the left, the shaft headframe center, Supply Depot and Upgrades to the right). Standing on a stall's column opens its menu; walking away closes it.
  • Hardware Store: sells bunker stock with fixed vibe prices, level locks, and owned plus deployed caps. The first catalog is Wall, Floor, Roof, Door, Floor Spikes, and the level 2 Basic Turret.
  • Supply Depot: dynamite, rope, and ladders at REQ-016/REQ-020 prices, packed straight into the current trip (Q-008 rule B). The sheet separates "have" counts from selected buy quantity, with quick buys for 1, 5, or 10 items at a time.
  • Upgrades: the permanent gear tracks; if the current surface trip has mined actions, the purchase banks that trip first, then applies the upgraded gear immediately to the fresh trip.
  • Without storage the stalls stay browsable with an honest "ledger offline" line; nothing is charged.
  • Shop feel (user-directed 2026-06-12): stall menus are mobile bottom sheets that slide up with thumb-sized rows (icon tile, name, blurb, owned or level badge, big price button) and the wallet in the header; buildings sit back in depth with a lit doorstep so the miner stands at the door instead of clipping the facade, and the door lights while the shop is open.
  • Destination buildings (user-directed 2026-06-16): the surface village is the overworld hub. Two buildings stand at the edges of the row, a Workshop on the far left and a Battles arena on the far right; standing on one shows a floating "Enter" button that routes to that screen instead of opening a stall sheet. Every entered screen returns with a "Back to mine" button, the top nav bar is gone from every page, and the landing page redirects to the mine.
  • Buying robot parts moved into the Workshop (user-directed 2026-06-16): the standalone Part Shop page is gone. Gear is bought at the Upgrades stall and supplies at the Supply Depot, so the in-Workshop shop is parts only; vibes come from reaching the surface with mine haul.
  • Offscreen base return (user-directed 2026-06-17): when the miner is on the surface and no village building is visible, an animated edge indicator points back toward the village. Tapping it opens a Base return menu with a small distance-scaled vibe cost, disables purchase when the wallet is short or offline, confirms before spending, then drops the miner at the shaft center with teleport SFX and a burst effect. Reaching the surface is the sell boundary, so base return never forces a mine checkpoint; it only charges and repositions after any surface auto-sell has already been triggered by coming up from below ground.

Trip-decision legibility (REQ-017)

  • The HUD shows the bag as carried resources, not currency, plus an estimated return cost in battery charge for the climb home. Surface sale feedback explains how many vibes those resources pay when sold; the battery readout warns when charge approaches the return cost (F-012's fix).
  • The collapse rules are stated in-world before the first descent (one line on the surface), not discovered by losing.

Dig feel and fanfare (REQ-018, REQ-019)

  • Render-layer juice, never sim: crack states on multi-hit blocks, debris particles and shake scaled to hardness, short smooth miner and camera tweens that settle before the next held repeat (no teleporting), action-specific SFX, pitch-ramped pickup chimes, slow-mo sting and banner on part-cache finds.
  • Collapse is a drama beat (battery warning, dim, "the crew hauls you up"), and every failed trip ends with a near-miss reveal: what you were carrying and the treasure that was almost in reach (the client has the full grid; pure UI).

The persistent endless mine (REQ-026, REQ-027)

  • The mine never resets (user-directed 2026-06-12: "it doesn't feel rewarding to see the mine reset every time I sell"). Auto-selling at the surface banks credits and parts but the carved world stays: every tunnel, ladder, plank, and structure persists forever. The trail IS the trophy (SteamWorld Dig 2 lesson).
  • The world is horizontally endless and vertically bounded by a hard floor: generation stays a pure function of (seed, col, row) across all columns and rows 0-999, the village anchors at the origin columns, and row 1000 is an impenetrable metal barrier across every column. The player cannot dig it, blast it, ride an elevator through it, warp past it, or fall through it.
  • Authored horizontal biome bands override the default mine style in their columns. The winter band spans columns -100 through -50 with snowy dirt, icy rocks, and cold ore variants. The high-tech band spans columns 100 through 150 with circuit terrain, wire palettes, and gadget resources that sell for vibes like ore. Outside those ranges, the mine keeps the default terrain and ore tables. The visible surface always keeps a top layer: grass in the default biome, snow in winter, and metal plating in high-tech columns.
  • Persistence model (Q-009 default): the world state is a sparse diff over pure generation (dug cells plus placed structures); the server stores the diff per player and replays each trip's action log on top of it to credit cash-outs, so determinism and anti-cheat survive without replaying a lifetime log. Guests persist the diff locally and adopt it server-side on first cash-out.
  • A trip is now surface-to-surface; consumables are bought and carry over per descent, not per world. The free ladder/plank refill is a death-recovery, not a per-trip grant (user-directed 2026-06-15).

The transport ladder (REQ-028 elevator, REQ-029 teleporter)

  • Progression arc: ladders and planks carry the early game; the elevator compresses the mid-game commute; range-limited warp beacons compress the late game; each tier is a visible investment in the world.
  • Elevator (REQ-028): one per player, an elevator tower on its own surface column beside the village. Depth is bought in segments at the tower (superlinear pricing per stratum reached); building it carves and rails its column. If a rail segment passes through existing laddered or planked cells in that column, those supports are removed from the world and returned to inventory. A one-time account cleanup scans already owned rail spans and returns any ladder or plank that was previously hidden behind the elevator. Riding is free, logged, and automatic from any floor on the built rail: starting a ride sends the miner to the owned depth or surface without repeated button presses. Each logged ride step still travels a fixed number of rows so the descent is visible, but the UI chains those steps until the endpoint. The base car covers 6 rows per step, and Elevator Speed increases both rows per step and automatic step cadence so the path still scales to a 1000-row rail. Ride-down clears the elevator column and stops at the owned depth; ride-up banks the carry when the car lands topside. The shaft is part of the persistent world.
  • Teleporter (REQ-029): late game. Beacon kits are consumables (priced like big dynamite); placing one plants a beacon at the miner's current underground cell, and a player can own at most two total beacons across packed kits and planted anchors. The village warp pad shows a scrollable newest-first list of planted beacons with short custom names; choosing one warps there for free as a logged coordinate action while the beacon's depth is within the warpcoil range. Standing on any in-range beacon can warp home and bank like surfacing. Edit pickup mode can salvage a visible adjacent beacon for carried partial vibe value, removing it from the world and from the pad list without returning a kit. Authored biome portal beacons sit on the surface in the winter and high-tech bands; activating one makes it a permanent free portal back to base and adds it to the village warp pad. The warpcoil is a persistent gear track upgraded over time (range tiers roughly 60, 150, 400, 1000 rows) for player-placed beacon depth, while activated biome portals are authored surface links.
  • Transport never skips unconquered depth: elevators only descend on the built rail to owned depth, beacons only plant where you stand. Range and segment pricing keep depth earned.

Deeper dangers and richer rewards (REQ-030)

  • Strata continue past the Magma Verge (Ashfall Galleries, the Black Seam, Echo Vaults, Core Approach, ...), each with palettes, Stamp Book goals, and a new pressure: magma pockets (vent like gas but burn triple), rock tier 4+ gating pickaxe 5+, denser hazard rolls.
  • Part caches scale with depth: deeper bands roll from richer part tables, so the bot-building reward track (REQ-007) keeps paying the deeper you push.

The no-softlock floor (REQ-031)

  • A broke miner is never stuck: a new player is gifted a starting ladder/plank bundle (8/4) once, Abandon is always available below ground (free, forfeits only the carry), and dying refills the ladder/plank stock up to 8/4 for free, so a player who runs dry can always surface and recover (user-directed 2026-06-15 moved the recurring free stock from a per-trip grant to a death-recovery, keeping only the one-time starting gift). Being out of money is a setback measured in bought ladders or one dead-battery refill, never a dead account.
  • Legibility: the Supply Depot states that ladders and planks cost vibes and that dying refills them, and a zero-ladders surface state surfaces a toast pointing at the depot and the death-refill.

What this does NOT cover yet

  • Smelt combos (consecutive matching ores multiply value), surface accrual between trips, non-mining collections, disguised hazard blocks beyond gas, server-issued seeds (F-011).
  • Music and authored audio assets stay outside scope for now; mining action SFX are synthesized in the client.
