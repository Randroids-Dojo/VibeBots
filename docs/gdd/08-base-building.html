Base Building and Bunker Defense

Status: partial

Spec

Base building is a mining-adjacent game mode that turns a persistent underground claim into a defended home. The first slice starts small: the player clears a large underground area, stakes one bunker claim, places purchased consumable building parts, and triggers a short autonomous Clanker attack that rewards survival. The long-term feature grows into a multi-level bunker builder, repeatable defense-event ladder, and primary path for overall player level.

Claim loop (REQ-034)

  • The player must physically clear a sufficiently large connected underground area before staking a bunker claim.
  • One player has one active bunker claim at first. The claim lives inside that player's persistent mine context.
  • The initial claim is a fixed rectangular footprint aligned from the miner's current cell: the miner stands on the bottom row at the horizontal midpoint when the bunker is placed. Later versions can support irregular rooms, room tags, and multiple outposts.
  • Claim staking is an explicit action with a preview overlay. The overlay is visualization only: it does not spawn a 7x5 layout of placed boxes and has no collision. The claim outline can be shown while the player is inside the claim.

Building parts (REQ-035)

  • Base parts are consumable inventory items purchased with vibes and later found through mining caches, defense rewards, and level unlocks.
  • The first Hardware Store catalog proves the loop with Wall, Floor, Roof, Door, Floor Spikes, and the level 2 Basic Turret. Later sets add hatches, stronger turrets, repair plates, traps, fixtures, and skins.
  • New bunker claims grant enough real starter parts to fully enclose the player cell in a small sealed room on day one: 6 walls, 4 floors, 4 roofs, and 1 door (a sealed 3x3 room takes 8 of the 15, leaving spares for layering or a roomier shape). There is no generic box building part.
  • Base parts render as riveted gunmetal steel in the shelter-cutaway style (light diffuse steel plates that read under lamp light, dark seam insets whose half-width edges complete the neighbor's joint, proud rivets, walkway grates, warm under-mounted room lamps on roofs, a porthole hatch door with an orange spin-wheel, safety-orange hazard accents) and the sealing parts fill their cell exactly to the shared edges, so adjacent cells tile seamlessly: stacked walls read as one bulkhead, floor rows as one deck, roof rows as one ceiling. Part geometry is cached per part and tier and drawn with the shared surface material singletons, so placing parts creates no new geometry, materials, or shader programs.
  • Basic Turrets unlock at player level 2, cost 160 vibes, and cap at one total owned or deployed. Surviving Clankers wear turret durability by one hit each, and the turret breaks when its fixed durability reaches zero.
  • Floor Spikes are consumable trap parts. Each raid step trigger damages one Clanker, lowers spike durability, and removes the spike once its fixed durability reaches zero.
  • Floor Spikes cap at 4 total owned or deployed at level 1 and 6 total owned or deployed from level 2.
  • Placement is cell-based. Parts snap to the bunker grid, consume one owned item, and carry local durability.
  • Removing an undamaged part while not under attack returns it to inventory.
  • Building is embodied in the mine instead of driven by an editor panel. Inside a claim, selecting a part from the compact toolbelt turns the existing mine direction input into an eight-direction build cursor. Releasing the direction sends the miner across the claim, into cardinal hammer range, without a separate Swing button. (Transitional: the 2026-07-13 first-person design pass replaces this flow with in-person building once that mode is solid on phones.)
  • A placed part grows into the target cell over four visible hammer strikes, reversing the mine cell damage rhythm. Pry uses the same targeting and approach flow to lift a placed part for relocation or stowing while preserving its durability. Deselecting the part immediately restores ordinary mine movement.
  • Selecting a build tool deploys a temporary visible scaffold throughout the claim. It lets the miner traverse empty claimed cells horizontally and vertically while approaching the target. Deselecting safely stows the scaffold.

Defense event (REQ-036)

  • The player can trigger a bunker defense event from a valid claimed bunker.
  • The first attack lasts roughly 3 minutes. Higher tiers later extend toward 5 minutes and spawn stronger or more numerous enemies.
  • The event is autonomous once started. Clankers path toward the bunker, chew through blockers, surround the perimeter, breach base parts, and eventually attack the player in the full feature.
  • If an open route through bunker cells reaches the player cell, Clankers take that route before attacking nearby walls. Walls only take the hit first when the player cell is fully enclosed.
  • The first implementation resolves the layout deterministically server-side. Later slices add live enemy movement inside the mine scene.

Clankers (REQ-037)

  • Clankers are autonomous little spidery robots that walk the mine grid, crawl through tunnels, chew through cells, and coordinate around bunker faces.
  • They should read differently from combat bots: low to the ground, many-legged, noisy, industrial, and pressure-driven.
  • Strength scales through health, chew rate, move cadence, armor, special role, spawn count, and event duration.

Rewards and player level (REQ-038)

  • Surviving a triggered defense event drops defense XP pickups toward overall player level; the miner must walk over the pickups before the XP can be claimed. Survived raids also grant vibes and later deterministic unlock progress.
  • The first level slice caps players at Level 2. Level 2 requires 100 defense XP from survived bunker attacks and raises the owned beacon cap from 2 to 3.
  • The overall player level gates the progression systems that proved fun in play: the raid tier a player may trigger, the owned beacon cap, and base-part owned limits. Robot parts, skins, consumables, and gear upgrades stay vibes-priced, because their prices already pace the shops and a retroactive level lock would take purchases away from existing players. New top-tier catalog entries may opt into a level floor as they are added (Q-017 option C, dev direction 2026-07-17).
  • Death during an attack grants no win reward. If a Clanker reaches the player cell, it self-destructs, kills the miner, ends the raid, and clears all XP pickups before collection. Later versions may add consolation stamps or repair discounts.

First-person bunker mode (design pass 2026-07-13)

  • The bunker claim is a 7x5x5 volume: the familiar 7-wide by 5-tall footprint in the tunnel plane (depth 0) plus four more layers into the claim rock (depths 1-4). Placed parts carry a depth coordinate; everything placed before this pass sits at depth 0. (The core, which also carried a depth, was removed in F-118.)
  • The interior starts as solid claim rock and is excavated cell by cell from inside. Claim rock is not chewable by Clankers, so un-dug rock naturally shields the back of existing sealed bunkers. Excavation costs no battery and cannot be undone in v1. (As shipped, F-115/F-116: the whole volume including the depth-0 plane starts solid except a small pre-mined spawn pocket, and excavation pays ore from the mine's own block generator, superseding both "depth 0 is open" and "yields nothing".)
  • Building happens in first person: from inside the claim the player enters a first-person view of the bunker interior only, walks with gravity and jumping, digs rock, and places or pries parts at a crosshair. This replaces the toolbelt cursor flow once the mode is solid on phones. (As shipped: raids are first-person only too, F-092, so the 2D mine sheet keeps only claiming, repairs, skins, and reset.)
  • Walking inside is free: no energy cost and no move-log actions; the miner's mine-grid position freezes at the entry cell while inside.
  • Raids become live and first-person, and both the bunker's layout and the player's movement decide the outcome (Q-024 option D, dev direction 2026-07-15). The player fights the raid from inside and runs around freely; leaving mid-raid is allowed but forfeits it (F-160/F-162), so staying and winning is the only way to a survival reward. Clankers approach and eat through the blocks around the base to breach it, each bite spending energy; a Clanker with energy left keeps pushing in 3D through the dug halls toward the player's live cell, spends more energy destroying walls in the way, and loses extra energy crossing spikes. A Clanker whose energy runs out dies where it stands (animated). If even one Clanker reaches the live player, the miner dies, the raid is lost, the death is animated, and a lost dialogue resolves back at the surface. Surviving means using the layout and dodging to drain every Clanker before one touches you. XP pickups drop and collect in 3D. (As shipped: the live first-person raid replaced the interim depth-0 projection entirely, F-092, and voluntary exit is now allowed but always forfeits, F-160/F-162, rather than trapping the player until the raid ends.)
  • The live raid runs on the client and the server trusts a bounded outcome rather than replaying it (the dev deliberately declined the heavy anti-cheat: with a handful of alpha players the only exploit is winning your own defense). The client simulates the raid in real time with the player's movement mattering, then reports the outcome (defense wear, loot, XP, win or loss). The server owns the lifecycle around that report: it freezes a start snapshot of the bunker and locks it against edits, applies the reported outcome against that snapshot with light bounds (within the authored duration, damage no worse than the snapshot's defenses allow), settles exactly once, and owns expiry so a disconnect resolves as a forfeit loss. No player movement trace and no browser/Node determinism parity are required, because the client is authoritative over movement and the server never re-runs it.

Minecraft-style bunker (design pass 2026-07-13, requested)

A player playtest of the first-person bunker asked for it to feel like Minecraft: real mineable blocks, a room you clearly stand inside, and a thin modular build kit. This pass evolves the first-person bunker toward that and supersedes the interim decisions in the bullets above. As shipped: excavation now pays ore rather than yielding nothing (F-116); raids moved fully into first person, so the 2D sheet keeps only claiming, repairs, skins, and reset (F-092); and the bunker core is removed, so its former center cell is ordinary buildable/diggable space (F-118). Tracked as F-114 through F-120; shipped one slice at a time under the resolved decisions in OPEN_QUESTIONS (Q-021 battery, Q-022 part migration).

  • Real mineable blocks (F-116, F-120). The claim's rock generates from the mine's own cell generator at the claim depth: full 3D blocks with the same kinds, break counts, and ore. Mining a bunker block drops 3D ore into the shared bag with normal capacity rules. Mining inside the bunker is free, no battery cost (Q-021 resolved B): the claim is a bounded number of blocks, so the bag capacity is the only limit. Reset and retry paths preserve resource depletion so that bounded volume cannot be regenerated for repeated rewards.
  • Minecraft mining feel (F-114). A first-person pickaxe swings and strikes the targeted block, holding keeps swinging and breaking with a cracking-progress overlay, reach is about three cells, and dragging the aim to another in-range cell mid-hold moves the strike there.
  • Spawn into a room (F-115, F-120). A fresh or reset claim pre-excavates a 3x3x3 pocket around the spawn so the player stands grounded inside an obvious room surrounded by diggable rock, instead of the flat 7x5x1 plane or inside solid rock.
  • Thin modular parts (F-117). Floors are thin slabs you walk over; walls are thin dividers on a cell boundary, rotatable, up to four per cell; floors build at any level as long as they attach to something solid on any face (rock, the boundary shell, a wall or door divider, a stair, or another placed part; dev direction 2026-07-19, superseding the earlier two-wall overhead rule) and fall when every attachment is destroyed; roofs are thin slabs only in the volume's topmost row (a roof is the bunker's outer ceiling, so a room that ends mid-column is capped with a second-story floor whose deck doubles as the lower room's ceiling, not with a roof; Q-025 resolved); rotatable modular staircases climb between layers. Parts occupy sub-cell slots, not whole cells, so one cell can hold several parts. Existing bunkers fail fast as incompatible and offer a confirmed Start fresh hard reset, with no migration or refund plumbing (Q-022 resolved: only two alpha players).
  • No core (F-118). The central core (pink octahedron) is removed; raids hunt the player cell only, matching the live first-person raid direction. Under Q-024 option D the hunted cell is the player's live position (the client runs the raid, so the wave chases where the player actually is), and reaching it kills the miner and loses the raid. No fixed target or core is needed.
  • Tighter UI (F-119). After a claim exists the flat view keeps one Enter bunker affordance; raid, repair, skin, and reset controls move behind a single in-bunker toggle menu reachable from inside first person.

Growth plan

  1. Prototype claim: detect a cleared underground rectangle, preview the claim, stake one bunker, and persist the footprint.
  2. Prototype builder: sell a tiny base-parts kit, place and remove blocks on the bunker grid, and show durability.
  3. Prototype raid: spawn simple Clankers outside the claim, path toward the bunker, chew blockers, and run a short survival timer.
  4. Reward ladder: add defense XP, player level, tier selection, and level gates for first base parts and bot parts.
  5. Full bunker feature: add multi-level layouts, doors, hatches, turrets, repairs, traps, specialist Clankers, base skins, stamps, and production-quality playtest evidence.
