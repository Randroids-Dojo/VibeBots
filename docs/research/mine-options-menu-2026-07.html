Research: Mine Options Menu

Date: 2026-07-30. User-directed: "can we actually research and redesign the options menu. it should probably utilize folders / nesting for better organization".

Follows the HUD redesign in docs/research/mine-hud-redesign-2026-07.html. That work left the settings gear as the single piece of persistent top-right chrome; this is about what opens when it is tapped.

1. What the menu is today

One flat scroll of ten leaves, rendered inline in mine-panel.tsx, with explanatory paragraphs interleaved between buttons:

The problem is not the count, it is that nothing is adjacent to anything related. Progression sits next to save state sits next to attribution sits next to a developer bench. There is no grouping, no hierarchy, and two of the ten carry multi-line body copy that pushes the rest below the fold, so the panel scrolls on a phone and its last items are reachable only by dragging. The 2026-07-30 layering bug (a rotating tip and the Jump button painting over the options) was made worse by exactly that height.

2. What the research says about nesting

  • Broadest first, increasing granularity. A multilevel menu should show the widest categories at the top level and narrow through submenus, and the hierarchy has to match the player's mental model rather than the code's structure.
  • Beware nested-doll navigation. Nielsen Norman Group's specific warning: menus that make users take a sequence of selections without showing useful information along the way are a pattern mobile users dislike. Depth is a cost, not a feature.
  • Information scent beats brevity. Long or nested menus need icons and supporting text so a row advertises what is behind it. A folder labelled only "More" is the failure case.
  • Games specifically: group related items and nest secondary features (settings, support, stats) behind a gear rather than spreading them across the main screen.

Read together, those give the constraint this design has to satisfy: nesting is allowed to remove scrolling, but every top-level row must say what is inside it, and no player should ever make two decisions to reach something they use often.

3. The design

Decisions taken with the owner on 2026-07-30: nested folders with drill-down, and developer-facing entries grouped under Advanced rather than hidden behind a debug gate.

Top level: five rows, no scrolling

  1. Stamp Book (direct). The headline collection feature stays one tap. Burying a progression surface behind a folder to win tidiness is the trade the research warns against.
  2. Saves and account (folder, 2): Load game, Account.
  3. About and help (folder, 4): Release notes, Replay bunker tutorial, Feedback, Credits.
  4. Advanced (folder, 2): Holodeck, Performance telemetry.
  5. Update alerts (inline toggle). A switch, not a destination, and player-facing.

The rules that keep it honest

  • Exactly two levels, ever. Root and one folder. Anything that wants a third level is a sign the grouping is wrong.
  • No folder holds fewer than two items. A one-item folder is a tap tax with no organising benefit. This is why "Notifications" is not a folder: update alerts is its only member, so it stays a top-level toggle.
  • Every folder row names its contents. Each carries a subtitle listing what is inside ("Release notes, tutorial, feedback, credits"), which is the direct answer to the nested-doll critique: the player can read the folder without opening it.
  • Toggles never nest for their own sake. Update alerts is player-facing and stays at root; performance telemetry is diagnostics and sits in Advanced. The split is by audience, not by control type.
  • Body copy belongs to its control. The two explanatory paragraphs move inside their folders, next to the toggle they describe, instead of pushing unrelated buttons below the fold at root.

Navigation and dismissal

  • Opening a folder replaces the panel contents; a back row at the top returns to root, labelled with the folder name so the player knows where they are.
  • Escape, gamepad cancel, and TV back step up one level first, and close the menu from root. That matches the platform expectation and means back never skips a level.
  • The menu reopens at root, never at the last folder: a menu that remembers its position surprises the next visit.
  • Dismissal otherwise keeps the existing contract (tap outside, the shared dismissal marker).

4. What this fixes

  • Root goes from ten items plus two paragraphs to five rows, which fits without scrolling on a 390x844 phone. That also removes the height that made the layering bug so visible.
  • Related things become adjacent: saves with account, attribution with version history, the QA bench with the diagnostics toggle.
  • The developer surfaces stop sitting beside a progression feature, without being hidden from the owner.
  • Adding an option later has an obvious home, instead of being appended to a flat list.

5. Risks and constraints

  • Drill-down costs a tap for six of the ten leaves. Mitigated by keeping the two most-used destinations (Stamp Book, and Load game one level down) shallow, but it is a real trade the owner accepted.
  • The e2e suite reaches these items by role and name. Several specs click "Release notes", "Account", "Holodeck", and the two toggles straight from the open menu; each now needs its folder opened first. That wants a helper in tests/e2e/support/mine-helpers.ts, in the same shape as openScrapMode, rather than the folder tap being repeated at every call site.
  • Rule 10 dismissal coverage applies: the back step and the outside-tap path both need smoke coverage, and the menu must stay above the HUD chrome (the HUD_LAYER.menu level added on 2026-07-30).
  • Not in scope: the destinations themselves. Stamp Book, Account, and the save-slot popup keep their current internals; this is the menu that opens them.
