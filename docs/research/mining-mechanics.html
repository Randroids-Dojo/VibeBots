Research: Mining Game Mechanics

Date: 2026-06-11. Web research across wikis, postmortems, design analyses, and reviews for Motherload/Super Motherload, Pocket Mine 1-3, Mr. Mine, Deep Town, SteamWorld Dig 1 and 2, Dome Keeper, Mr. Driller, Idle Miner Tycoon, Mineblast!!, Terraria, plus game-feel and retention-psychology sources. Feeds the mining spec in docs/gdd/06-mining.html.

Game profiles

Motherload / Super Motherload

  • Core loop: fly a pod down a 2D grid, dig, fill cargo, return to surface to sell, refuel, repair, upgrade. Trip ends by choice (cargo full, fuel low) or death (fuel empty explodes the pod).
  • Fuel is the trip clock. Starting tank (10 L) lasts about 30 seconds. Tank tiers: 10, 15 ($750), 25 ($2,000), 40 ($5,000), 60 ($20,000), 100 ($100,000), 150 L ($500,000). Capacity grows ~15x while price grows ~667x: late tiers are prestige sinks.
  • Ten ore tiers, exponential value: Ironium $30, Bronzium $60, Silverium $100, Goldium $250, Platinum $750, Einsteinium $2,000, Emerald $5,000, Ruby $20,000, Diamond $100,000, Amazonite $500,000 (roughly 2-5x per tier). Heavier minerals slow the pod, so the richest cargo is the hardest to haul home.
  • Hazards by depth band: undiggable stone ~1,500 ft, lava pockets ~3,000 ft, gas pockets ~4,750 ft, fall damage. Hazards disguise as ordinary blocks, so depth knowledge becomes skill.
  • Consumables priced as insurance: Reserve Fuel ~$2,000, Dynamite ~$2,000, Plastic Explosive ~$5,000, Hull Nanobots ~$7,500, Quantum Teleporter $2,000, Matter Transmitter $10,000 (teleport home with cargo).
  • Dopamine: depth milestone story beats with cash gifts (-1,000 ft pays $3,000), random artifacts below 950 ft, hidden unique upgrades found (not bought) between 4,000 ft and the core.
  • Super Motherload adds consecutive-collection smelting combos (order of pickup multiplies value): route order becomes a scoring layer, fully deterministic.

Pocket Mine 1-3 (mobile)

  • One-thumb, sub-minute runs. Tap blocks to break; pickaxe durability is the energy budget (PM2 starts ~200 hits; PM3 ~50 effective digs per round). Everything auto-banks: loss is impossible, tension is spending durability wisely.
  • Special blocks carry the texture: bomb crates, explosive gas, ore boulders that clear neighbors, fireworks, bank blocks, rails, chain lightning. Chain reactions cost zero durability: spotting one is found money.
  • Meta: durability upgrades, card loadouts (use-once with cooldown timers), pets, per-world artifact collections that pay completion rewards.
  • Reviews call the tap-to-dig one-input model the mobile-native standard; its weakest element is reflex combat.

Mr. Mine (idle)

  • Depth milestones gate all content: named unlocks at exact depths (Caves 45 km, Scientists 50 km, Chest Collector 100 km, Broken Robot 225 km, Underground City 300 km), 2,500+ km total. The depth number itself is the progress bar.
  • Lesson: publish the depth ruler. Named strata, milestone unlocks, and a visible deepest-depth stat are cheap and drive long-horizon goals.

Deep Town (mobile idle)

  • The mine is a vertical stack of floors, 9 segments per floor, with a boss block at the bottom of each area. Blocks get more resilient with depth.
  • Raw ore is worth little; processed goods are worth much more. Value comes from transformation, not just extraction.
  • Lesson: strata with a capstone block at the bottom of each band give each depth band a finale. Maps directly onto a 9-wide grid.

SteamWorld Dig 1 and 2

  • Dig down, fill limited loot sacks, climb back, sell, upgrade. Trip enders: inventory full, light low, health low, or stuck.
  • The developers' published deep dive: "keeping the player in constant risk of getting stuck" was the deliberate primary tension; digging destroys the terrain you climb on. Playtesters hated stuck-with-no-out, so wall-jump was added as the skill escape valve. Punishing dead-ends need an escape valve (skill or consumable) or the tension reads as unfair.
  • Light is a soft timer: darkness makes everything risky rather than killing you; ore sparkles in the dark, so darkness hides value.
  • Upgrades unlock verbs, not just numbers: drill tiers gate tougher rock; dynamite, super jump, wall-jump change traversal. Story characters pay cash gifts at depth thresholds.

Dome Keeper

  • Between monster waves, dig and haul resources home; the wave timer forces a "one more vein or run home" call every 2-3 minutes inside 20-30 minute runs.
  • Carry capacity is the central decision: you start carrying a couple of units and they slow you; veins are bigger than your capacity. Three resources with three jobs (iron upgrades, water gadgets, cobalt repairs): the designers call two too flat and four overload.
  • Resource abundance grows with depth, so depth is always tempting. "Hundreds of micro-decisions" given meaning by the clock.

Mr. Driller

  • Fixed-width well, blocks fall when unsupported and crush you; 4+ same-color blocks auto-clear, causing chains. Air depletes constantly, refilled by capsules in the field.
  • Lesson: support rules (unsupported blocks fall after a beat) are a fully deterministic, grid-native hazard producing dodges, near-misses, and self-inflicted disasters with zero physics engine.

Idle Miner Tycoon, Mineblast!!, Terraria, Wall Kickers, juice canon

  • Idle Miner Tycoon: always one obvious next purchase (bottleneck-widening); reported D1 retention ~75% vs ~40% industry norm. Returning should feel like opening a present.
  • Mineblast!!: placing a bomb and watching a quadrant erupt is the juiciest verb in the genre.
  • Terraria: pickaxe-power hard gates (a pickaxe below threshold does nothing to the block); cavern layer has 4-5x surface ore density; this tier's ore builds the pickaxe for the next tier. Cleanest hard-gate progression in the genre.
  • Wall Kickers reviews credit one input with analog consequence, instant restart, 1-2 minute runs. The animation layer must feel analog even when the sim is discrete.
  • Juice canon (Juice It or Lose It, Vlambeer): crack states, debris particles, shake scaled to event size, pitch-ramped pickup sounds, pause-frames on big finds; reserve the big effects for rare events so they keep meaning. Near-miss psychology: losses that almost won activate reward circuitry; show what the player almost got.

Cross-game patterns

  • Trip anatomy: one primary trip clock plus one or two secondary caps (fuel + cargo + hull; durability + scroll; wave timer + carry). Trips end well when the player chose to come home and memorably when a gamble failed; both endings must be possible.
  • Push-your-luck formula: value density rises with depth, return cost rises with depth, unbanked value is at risk. The strongest lever is carried-loot-lost-on-death, banked-loot-safe. Insurance consumables monetize the tension without removing it.
  • Ore tiers: 6-10 tiers, exponential value (2-5x per step), rarity inverse to value, depth-banded with overlap so the previous tier remains one band deeper.
  • Upgrades: tracks for dig strength (gates hard rock), energy, cargo, mobility, hull, light/information. Costs steeply superlinear (x2.5-x5 per tier). The best upgrades unlock verbs or access. Players should hit a visible wall one band before they can afford the answer.
  • Hazards: mobile-friendly hazards are informational and positional, not reflexive: disguised damage blocks learned by depth, unsupported-block falls with a warning beat, undiggable stone forcing detours. Reflex enemies port badly to one-thumb play.
  • Consumables: burst clearance (dynamite), escape insurance (reserve fuel, teleporter), information (scanner). Each resolves one dread; priced at ~10-30% of an average trip haul.
  • Movement: gravity-walkers (dig is irreversible terrain editing) generate better grid decisions than free flight; one-thumb standard is tap-the-adjacent-block.
  • Pacing: trips of under a minute (Pocket Mine) or decision points every 2-3 minutes (Dome Keeper). Dopamine schedule: guaranteed small payoff every few seconds, probable mid payoff per trip, rare jackpot every 5-20 trips with full fanfare.
  • Meta: deepest-depth stat, named strata, collections with completion rewards, milestone gifts, daily/cooldown hooks, always-one-affordable-upgrade pacing.

Transport and persistence progression (2026-06-12 pass)

Second research pass for the endless-mine epic: how the genre keeps deep runs rewarding once ladders stop scaling, and why the world must persist.

  • Mr. Mine: one persistent ever-deepening shaft; the elevator IS the game camera, and depth milestones every few dozen km gate entire new systems (50km golem, 100km chest collector, 501km core, new worlds at 1032km). Lesson: a permanent world plus depth-gated unlock cadence is the retention spine; nothing resets, so every meter dug is banked progress.
  • Motherload: consumable matter transporters teleport home from depth; fuel pressure makes the return trip the tension. Lesson: late transport tools convert "can I make it back" anxiety into an economy without deleting it; our beacon kits play this role.
  • SteamWorld Dig 2: pneumatic tubes bought at depth landmarks give permanent fast travel through a persistent hand-dug world; players cite the carved-out map as the game's pride artifact. Lesson: visible permanent tunnels are themselves the reward; fast travel anchors live IN the world the player carved.
  • Deep Town: portals/teleports unlock by depth bands and shrink the commute, while mining areas persist and automate behind you. Lesson: transport upgrades should compress already-conquered space, never skip unconquered space: range limits do that.
  • Terraria: the world is permanent and shafts/hellevators are long-term infrastructure investments. Lesson: infrastructure built with consumables (ropes, platforms) graduates into engineered permanence (hellevators, teleporters) as wealth grows: the exact ladder, elevator, teleporter arc.

Synthesis for VibeBots: persist the claim forever (the carved trail is the trophy), keep generation pure so endlessness is free, and ladder the transport economy: free ladder/plank provisions floor the early game, a purchasable elevator compresses the mid-game commute along one shaft, and range-limited beacons+warp compress the late game anywhere, upgraded over time. Depth milestones (strata first-reach bonuses, richer caches) supply the unlock cadence.

Prioritized mechanics for VibeBots (impact over implementation cost)

Items marked [OMT] are primary "one more trip" compulsion drivers. All are table-driven or resolve as discrete grid steps, so server replay of the move log reproduces every payout exactly; juice lives entirely in the render layer.

  1. [OMT] Exponential depth-banded ore tiers (7-9 tiers, ~3x value per tier, overlapping bands).
  2. [OMT] Carried-vs-banked loot with loss on collapse plus a visible carried-value HUD counter: the number on screen is the bet.
  3. [OMT] Energy as the single trip clock with return cost rising with depth, surfaced to the player.
  4. Block-break juice ladder: crack states, hardness-scaled particles and shake, pitch-ramped pickup audio, slow-mo plus sting on rare finds.
  5. Hard-rock gates keyed to pickaxe tier (the block does not break at all below the required tier).
  6. [OMT] Steep upgrade ladders on 4 tracks (pickaxe, energy, cargo, light) with superlinear costs (x2.5-x4 per tier).
  7. Unsupported-block falls resolved as discrete grid steps with a one-tick wobble warning.
  8. Insurance and burst consumables (bomb, emergency recall) priced at ~10-30% of an average trip haul.
  9. [OMT] Depth milestones: named strata, first-reach bonus and banner, persistent deepest-depth record.
  10. Chain-reaction blocks (gas pockets, ore boulders, crystal clusters): deterministic flood-fill.
  11. Disguised hazard blocks learned by depth band.
  12. [OMT] Robot-part caches as rare jackpots (one per 5-20 trips) with full fanfare, plus a collection screen.
  13. [OMT] Post-trip near-miss reveal: what you died carrying and what was almost in reach.
  14. Adjacency smelt combos (consecutive matching ores multiply value): route-ordering skill with zero new content.
  15. Surface accrual between trips (restocks, contracts): returning feels like opening a present.

The "one more trip" engine: the exponential ore curve makes the next band irresistible, the carried-value counter makes greed visible, the energy clock forces the bank-or-push call before the player is ready, milestone and upgrade thresholds are always one trip away, jackpot caches run on a variable-ratio schedule, and the near-miss reveal ends every failed trip with proof that success was two blocks away. Trips of 60-120 seconds keep the cost of "just one more" trivially low.
