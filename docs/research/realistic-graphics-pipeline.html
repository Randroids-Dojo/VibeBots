Research: Realistic Graphics Pipeline

User-directed 2026-07-02: reach the perceived quality of the viral "one-shot Age of Empires / Counter-Strike in three.js" demos. This document analyzes what actually makes those demos read as realistic, audits VibeBots' current render stack against it, and lays out a sliced roadmap that fits Rule 3 (no new core dependencies) and the mobile-first constraint (Android portrait, forced WebGL2 on coarse pointers).

What the viral demos actually do

The one-shot demos are three.js scenes whose realism comes overwhelmingly from the lighting and post pipeline, not from asset fidelity. Consistent ingredients:

  • Real-time shadows with soft filtering (PCF-soft or VSM), usually one sun-style directional light with a tight shadow camera.
  • Image-based lighting: an HDRI environment (or procedural sky) feeding PBR materials through PMREM, so every surface picks up sky color, bounce, and reflections.
  • An HDR post stack: bloom on emissives and bright speculars, ambient occlusion (GTAO/SSAO), temporal or SMAA anti-aliasing, filmic tone mapping (ACES or AgX), vignette, and color grading. Tone mapping alone is most of the "cinematic" impression.
  • Surface detail: PBR texture sets or procedural normal/roughness perturbation so light has something to catch. Geometry is often simple; the materials carry it.
  • Atmosphere: fog, god rays, dust particles, depth-of-field in vista shots.
  • They run on desktop WebGPU with no frame-rate promises: no phone budget, no CI, no determinism constraint. Reported by press as single-prompt outputs; at least one investigation (36kr) found some viral clips were partly handcrafted. The techniques are still exactly the list above.

Case study: Pat Simmons' Counter-Strike clone (the reference the user cited)

Pat Simmons (@per_simmons, aiformortals.co) published two videos: "I Made Opus 4.8 and Fable 5 Build the Same App (RAW RESULTS)" (e-commerce store, 3D art museum, Age of Empires-style RTS) and "Fable 5 Is BACK And It Just One Shotted THIS" (a Counter-Strike clone, deployed at fable5-cs27126.vercel.app). The CS build is publicly deployed, so its production bundle was inspected directly (2026-07-02). Findings:

  • Plain WebGLRenderer, not WebGPU. The entire look is achieved with the classic three.js stack that our WebGL2 fallback path equals or exceeds.
  • PCF-soft shadow mapping (VSM also referenced), one directional sun plus many spot/point lights with per-light shadow settings.
  • PMREM + RoomEnvironment: the built-in neutral studio environment used as the IBL source. Zero texture downloads; every PBR surface gets reflections and ambient response for free.
  • EffectComposer with SSAO and a bloom pass; AgX tone mapping (AgX references outnumber ACES in the bundle).
  • Exponential fog everywhere (FogExp2; ~73 fog references): the single cheapest source of depth and atmosphere.
  • Procedural textures: CanvasTexture/DataTexture generation with normal/roughness maps assigned in code; no .glb/.hdr/.png asset URLs appear in the bundle strings. The surface detail is generated, not downloaded.
  • InstancedMesh for repeated geometry.
  • Not actually one-shot: the video's own chapter list includes "Okay, Full Disclosure" and "3+ Hours of Non-Stop QA". The polish came from an iterative loop, which is exactly the loop this repo already runs.

Implication: the cited reference validates the roadmap below almost term for term, and none of it requires WebGPU-only features. Where he used classic EffectComposer passes, we use the three-native TSL pipeline (better: it targets both backends); where he used canvas-generated textures, we can generate detail in TSL directly on the GPU.

VibeBots today (audit, 2026-07-02)

  • Shadows: none. Zero castShadow/receiveShadow anywhere; the R3F canvases never enable renderer shadows.
  • Environment lighting: none. Ambient + hemisphere + one directional + the miner's point lamp. No environment map, so metals and crystals have nothing to reflect.
  • Post-processing: none. No composer/pipeline; emissive surfaces (ores, lamp, the new miner materials) never bloom.
  • Tone mapping: R3F default ACESFilmic. Never tuned; no exposure control.
  • Materials: flat-shaded MeshStandard across the mine; the miner just moved to TSL node materials with fresnel rims (PR #84), which proves the TSL path works on both backends.
  • Budget instrumentation: data-draw-calls and data-frame-ms on the mine canvas, data-holodeck-draw-calls on the bench; capture tooling from the character pipeline.

Conclusion: the gap to the demo look is not model quality; it is the absence of the entire lighting/post pipeline. Closing it is additive render-layer work with the stack we already pin.

What our pinned stack already ships (three 0.185.1, WebGPU + WebGL2 fallback)

All of the following exist as TSL nodes under three/examples/jsm/tsl/display/ and compile through the same node system as our miner materials, WebGPU-first with the WebGL2 fallback:

  • AA: TRAA, TAAU (temporal upscale), SMAA, FXAA, SSAA.
  • Light transport: GTAO, SSGI (screen-space global illumination), SSR, SSS, Godrays, Lensflare, ImportanceSampledEnvironment.
  • Camera/film: Bloom, DepthOfField, MotionBlur, Film grain, ChromaticAberration, Vignette-style grading via Lut3D.
  • Performance: FSR1 spatial upscaling (render at a fraction of native, upscale), Denoise/RecurrentDenoise for cheap AO/GI sampling.
  • Tone mapping: ACESFilmic and AgX built into the renderer.

No new dependencies are required for any of the roadmap below (Rule 3 holds; the pmndrs postprocessing ban stays irrelevant because everything is three-native TSL).

Constraints the demos do not have

  • Android portrait at 390x760 is the primary device, and coarse pointers force the WebGL2 backend. Every effect must have a phone tier or a clean off-switch. Temporal passes (TRAA/SSGI) and multi-tap AO are desktop-tier by default.
  • Determinism: render-layer only; nothing here touches src/sim. Screenshot-based tests must keep stable predicates (pixel tests already audited once for the miner materials; bloom will shift them and each slice budgets for test updates).
  • Draw calls and fill rate: dpr is capped at 2; post passes add full-screen fill cost, which is why FSR1/TAAU matter (render at ~0.7x on phones, upscale to native).
  • Art direction: the goal is a realistic RENDER of VibeBots' stylized world (lighting, shadow, atmosphere, material response), not swapping the art for photoreal assets. The palette and silhouettes stay; the light gets real.

Sliced roadmap

Each slice is one PR, tested on the Holodeck bench plus mine smokes, with captures on WebGPU and forced-WebGL2 at both viewports, and a quality-tier switch so phones always have an escape hatch. A graphicsQuality setting (auto/low/high) lands in G1 and every later slice keys off it.

  1. G1: Light and shadow foundation. Enable renderer shadows (PCF-soft; VSM if fallback behaves), sun directional with a tight shadow camera on the surface, the miner's lamp becomes a shadow-casting light underground with tier-scaled map size (512 phone / 2048 desktop), AgX tone mapping + exposure tuning, quality-tier plumbing and auto device detection. Biggest visual jump per millisecond spent.
  2. G2: Environment and material response. PMREM environment (small compressed HDRI or a procedural TSL sky per stratum), ores/metal/crystals get reflective response, drop flatShading on hero surfaces, procedural triplanar normal/roughness detail for dirt and rock via TSL noise (no texture downloads, works on both backends).
  3. G3: Post stack. three-native PostProcessing pipeline with tiers: phones get bloom + vignette/grade (+FXAA when needed); desktop adds GTAO (denoised), TRAA, god rays from the headlamp, subtle DoF on the Holodeck bench. FSR1 as the phone fill-rate escape hatch. The emissives authored in the miner materials start paying off everywhere.
  4. G4: Atmosphere and dressing. Depth fog tuned per stratum, dust motes upgraded to lamp-lit volumetric-feel billboards, surface village material pass, block-face micro-displacement variety.
  5. G5: Pipeline hardening. Capture tool grows A/B baseline diffing and per-tier capture matrices; perf gates on data-frame-ms/draw-calls in smoke; a Holodeck "Lighting Lab" scenario with light-rig and tier controls joins the Miner Showcase as the standing QA bench.
