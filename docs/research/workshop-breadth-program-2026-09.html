Workshop breadth and expression program

Written 2026-09-02 from the second Fun Factor Audit of the workshop garage program (docs/FUN_FACTOR_AUDIT.html). That audit found the first minute and the core loop at the bar the garage program set, and named what still keeps the workshop short of the session goal (Banjo-Kazooie Nuts and Bolts level building): breadth and expression. F-246 (the hour-in surprise: the catalog and the roster run out) and F-247 (skill expression: two builds of the same parts fight the same) are the program's two threads.

The brief and the bar

Nuts and Bolts keeps a builder busy for hours because every part changes how a vehicle behaves and the challenges keep asking for a different vehicle. VibeBots' bench has the first half in miniature (parts have mass, power, reach, and merge levels) and the sim already reads three temperament levers and gearing, but the loop never names them: the debrief points at parts and the Tune tab, the roster is four fixed fights, and the catalog tops out at three rungs. The bar for this program: a player an hour in still has a lever to learn, a fight that asks for a different bot, and a part to reach for.

What the sim already gives a build to vary

  • Temperament (botBehaviorSchema, design-owned, sim-read): aggression scales throttle 0.8x to 1.2x, patience scales the backoff cycle after closing 0.5x to 1.5x, flank bias scales the flank arc 0.5x to 1.5x. Neutral 0.5 reproduces the classic controller. The Tune tab has the three sliders.
  • Gearing (gearRatio on drive connections, fixed set): more torque or more speed on the wheels.
  • Orientation: quarter-turn yaw on placements (F-006), so a weapon can face the front or the side.
  • The match score (MatchScore) records damage, parts, health, mobility at the end, and pressure ticks (ticks spent closing on the opponent), so a decision can be read as who was on the front foot.

So F-247 as the audit stated it is too strong: two builds of the same parts can already fight differently. What is true is that nothing in the loop teaches the levers, so the player never finds them. H1 fixes that where the player is already listening: the debrief.

Principles for every slice

  • No sim change unless a slice says so; adding parts and opponents does not bump SIM_VERSION (existing matchups replay byte-identical), a new lever the sim reads does.
  • Every lever the debrief names has a one-tap fix that applies it and shows the player where it lives, so the next fight tests the lesson.
  • Every new opponent punishes a build the debrief can name a counter for; every new part is a reason to come back to the shop, not a recolour.
  • Rule 10 e2e per slice; captures into the review session; a Fun Factor Audit article at the end of the program.

Slice plan, in order

H1: The debrief teaches the levers

Two lessons that read the match score and the design's temperament. A decision lost while spending less of the fight on the front foot says so and offers Raise aggression (the throttle lever). A knockout taken while giving far less than it took says the bot stayed in the pocket and offers Raise patience (the reset lever). Both fix-it actions apply the change through setBehavior (undoable, +0.2 and +0.25 respectively, clamped) and open Tune so the player sees the slider move. The decision lesson keeps its old form (Open Tune) when the player was already on the front foot. Priority moves the decision lesson ahead of the soak lesson: on a decision, the judges' card is the verdict and the soak is a detail. Pure rules, unit tested; the G9 e2e grows to press Raise aggression and read the slider.

H2: The roster becomes a ladder (measured)

As first written this slice was an armoured hammer bot that would shrug off spikes. Measured before building (a headless resolveMatch matrix, 2026-09-02): every armoured variant, with drive, grip, or super wheels, a nose wedge, a hardened lid, and a hammer on the core or the lid, lost to the Cube Rammer, twice by knockout inside ten seconds; the damage model rewards reach and mobility, not plate. The same matrix showed the ladder already existed and was hidden: the Cube Rammer beats Brawler, Contagion, and Night Terror on the card, edges out to Bulldozer (176 to 173, a bench-only archetype the picker never offered), and loses decisively to Impaler (232 to 83); and a lance in the spike's place beats Impaler (270 to 32) and everything else, so the debrief's never-connected advice is right. The slice therefore ships FIGHT_LADDER: the five stock fights in measured order with a hint per rung, Bulldozer in the picker, and a unit test that fights the ladder and pins the outcomes so a sim change that flips them is caught. No new part and no armour archetype: that would need a damage model that rewards plate, which is F-247 territory.

H4: The top of the ladder (added from the closing audit)

The closing audit found the ladder too easy at the top once a build carried a lance. Measured 2026-09-02 (headless resolveMatch, eleven candidates against the spike, lance, and tempered-lance first builds, then the two strongest against nine buildable counters): the first matrix put candidates in the player seat and misled, because the spawn arrangement is not symmetric: the saw-on-ballast design that won there loses to the plain spike build from the opponent seat. Re-measured in the opponent seat, a patient horizontal bar on drive wheels with a ballast tail beats the spike (564 to 28), the lance (329 to 259), and the tempered lance (309 to 38), and loses to a tempered lance with a ballast tail (409 to 249), to a bar with a tail, and to a Bulldozer with a tail; the strongest candidate (a tempered lance on ballast wheels with a tail) beat almost everything and would have been a wall. Gravestone is therefore the sixth rung, with its counter named in its blurb, and the ladder test pins that the lance build beats Impaler, loses to Gravestone, and that the tempered-lance-and-tail build beats Gravestone.

H3: Catalog wave two

A fourth rung where a family has three, and one part that is not a rung: a heavier wheel above the Super Wheel for the tower builds that cannot turn, a tier-three spike (a tempered lance), and a counterweight block (structure, heavy, cheap) that lets a top-heavy build stop tipping. Follows the G4 pattern: additive parts, looks, blurbs, shop rows, chips, no sim version change.

Sequencing and gates

H1 first: it is the smallest and it is the expression thread. H2 second: it gives H1's lessons a fight to be right about. H3 last, then the audit. H4 was added after the audit named the top of the ladder as the next gap. Each slice: branch from the stack's top, verify, PR, bot review settled, and the owner merges the stack in order.
